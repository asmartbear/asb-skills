## What this is about

A small fraction of your customers make most of your profit. At WP
Engine, 5% of customers produced 40% of the profit. The book calls the
composite of these perfect customers **Carol**. The book makes a claim
that seems wrong at first, and gives two chapters of proof for it: if you
aim all your marketing, positioning, and product development at Carol
*alone*, your market does not shrink. You win the ring of buyers who share
her trade-offs — a ring ten times larger. You also win the ring of buyers
who weigh those trade-offs and pick you anyway — a ring one hundred times
larger. In 1994, Subaru targeted lesbians. Subaru then became the
fastest-growing car company of the next decade. Most of Subaru's new
buyers were not gay. Clear trade-offs, stated with confidence, win
everyone who leans your way.

You cannot invent Carol from wishes. You derive her, step by step, from
an honest look at who you actually are. Start with your real strengths
and real weaknesses. Refine these into the keystones that force a
purchase, the deal-breakers that block one, and the inciting events that
turn a someday-buyer into a today-buyer. This workshop comes from *Carol
Loves You for You* and *Speaking Carol Into Existence*, Chapters 2 and 3
of *[Hidden Multipliers](https://hiddenmultipliers.com)*. It runs this
process as six skills. Each skill produces a file. The next skill uses
that file. This way the work builds up. It does not disappear after a
whiteboard session.

## How to run it

1. **Get the raw facts on the record.**
   [Observations](/skills/asb-carol-observations/) walks you through twelve
   tough question categories. Or it processes your team's write-storm
   notes. The categories cover what customers praise, what makes them
   leave, what you claim but are not, and what you envy. The output is
   `OBSERVATIONS.md`: vivid, specific raw material, with no judgment yet.
2. **Distill and classify.**
   [Strengths & Weaknesses](/skills/asb-carol-strengths/) converts the
   observations into the deep-truth attributes behind them. It removes
   generic attributes with the Opposite Test. It classifies each attribute
   as a strength, a weakness, or both — `STRENGTHS-WEAKNESSES.md`.
3. **Refine strengths into keystones.**
   [Keystones](/skills/asb-carol-keystones/) asks one question for each
   strength: whose circumstances *require* an extreme version of this
   strength? A strength that names no real market segment is table stakes,
   not a keystone. The output is `KEYSTONES.md`.
4. **Refine weaknesses into deal-breakers.**
   [Deal-Breakers](/skills/asb-carol-dealbreakers/) maps who is
   disqualified, no matter how much they like you. This group is your
   anti-market. The skill uses these exclusions to sharpen the description
   of each keystone market. The output is `DEALBREAKERS.md`, plus an
   improved `KEYSTONES.md`.
5. **Find the inciting events.**
   [Inciting Events](/skills/asb-carol-inciting-events/) finds what moves a
   perfect-fit customer from "priority seven" to "buying today." When you
   have customer interviews, the skill pulls these events from them. When
   you do not, it works backward from the keystones. The output is
   `INCITING-EVENTS.md`.
6. **Define Carol.**
   [Define Carol](/skills/asb-carol-define/) combines everything into
   `CAROL.md`. This file gives a clear, behavioral definition of your ideal
   customer. It lists her attributes first, because that is the point. It
   also includes reference sections on keystones, deal-breakers, and
   inciting events. Positioning, marketing, and product work all start
   from this file.

The output feeds every step downstream. Positioning speaks Carol's
language. Marketing buys her attention. Sales qualifies leads against her
markers. Product builds what thrills her. If you want to *test* what you
have written, use the
[Customer Interviews](/workshops/customer-interviews/) workshop as a
companion. Its first goal question is "What does Carol look like?" Its
findings report feeds directly back into these files.
