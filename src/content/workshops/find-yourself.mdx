## What this is about

The right strategy for a product, a company, or a career depends on things you
already do very well. It must also be things you *want* to keep doing for
years. It is the game you are built to win. Competitors misjudge this
strategy because they judge the wrong player. But you cannot build a company
*in the shape of you* until you know your own shape. "Know thyself" has been
the hardest task for two and a half thousand years. You cannot read the label
from inside the jar. You are biased. You rehearse who you think you *should*
be. Your most powerful traits stay invisible to you because they come so
naturally. For example, an extrovert works a room well but genuinely cannot
explain "how."

This workshop comes from *To Thine Own Self Be True*, Chapter 11 of
*[Hidden Multipliers](https://hiddenmultipliers.com)*. It is the companion to
[Find Your Carol](/workshops/find-your-carol/). In that workshop, you built
the customer who is naturally perfect for your product. Here, you build the
strategy that is naturally perfect for *you*. This workshop runs as two
skills. The first skill draws an honest map of who you are. It records each
trait as a *fact*, not as a strength or a weakness — the effect of a trait
depends on the context ("Competitive" wins a sales call and poisons a team
meeting). The second skill distills that map down to the one or two traits.
These traits are so extreme, and so uniquely yours, that customers accept
every other trade-off. Each skill produces a file. The next step — and your
positioning, pricing, and product decisions — uses that file.

## How to run it

1. **Map who you actually are.**
   [Who, Me?](/skills/asb-who-me/) helps you get out of the jar. It uses drive
   prompts (*even as a kid I would ___*), anti-questions (*when I'm faced
   with ___, I feel it in the pit of my stomach*), and outside-in questions.
   You give these questions to people who know you. They ask about your
   perfect scenario, your personal hell, and the strengths you cannot see
   because they come so naturally. The skill presses every flattering label
   until it finds the real event behind it. It also welcomes unglamorous
   motives, such as money, fame, or proving a point. The result is a file
   named `WHO-ME.md`. Each finding is a fact about your personality, with the
   contexts where it helps and where it quietly causes harm.
2. **Distill to your voter(s).**
   [Voters](/skills/asb-voters/) takes that self-portrait and tests every
   candidate trait through a hard set of checks: extremity earned through
   obsession, rarity among your peers, decisiveness, and reverberation into
   product, pricing, and market. The skill allows at most two traits to pass.
   This is the debate tactic that wins a contest you are losing on points:
   one or two decisive strengths override every weakness, the way Steve
   Jobs's obsession with industrial design overpowered the weaknesses of
   iTunes. The output is a file named `VOTERS.md`. It lists at most two
   voters, with their evidence and downstream effects, plus the near-miss
   *special strengths* you can build on as secondary pillars — or, if nothing
   is extreme enough yet, an honest statement of that fact.

## What you get at the end

`VOTERS.md` is the seed of a strategy that competitors cannot copy. It grows
from an obsession they are not willing to match. It shapes the rest of your
decisions: your [positioning](/skills/asb-positioning/) leads with the voter,
your pricing strategy lives up to it, your product reverberates from it, and
your market loves you *for* it — even reading your weaknesses as proof of the
strength they came for.

This workshop also closes the loop with
[Find Your Carol](/workshops/find-your-carol/). Suppose you and your product
are in harmony, but the customer is not. Then, by definition, that customer
was not Carol. Re-run the Carol analysis with what you now know. Together, the
two "Who?" workshops define the two people every strategy depends on: your
ideal customer, and you.
