## What this is about

The right strategy — for a product, a company, or a career — depends
on things you already do exceptionally well. It also depends on
things you *want* to keep doing for years. But "know thyself" has
been a hard task for two and a half thousand years. You are biased.
You carry baggage. You have spent years acting out who you think you
should be. Meanwhile, the most powerful facts about you are often
invisible to you. For example, an extrovert moves through a party
with ease. The extrovert cannot explain "how," because for them it
is not a technique.

This skill helps you see what you cannot see about yourself. It walks
you through the book's self-discovery prompts (*even when I was a
kid, I would ___; whenever I ___, I get lost in the work and feel
energized when it ends*). Then it asks the anti-questions (*when I'm
faced with ___, I feel it in the pit of my stomach*). It also assigns
outside-in questions as homework for people who know you: the
perfect-scenario question, the personal-hell question, and the
question that finds your edge — *what are my strengths I can't see
because they come so naturally?*

This is a strict interrogation, not a survey. For every
self-flattering label, the skill asks for the real episode behind it.
For every polished motive, the skill asks one required follow-up
question: "what is the less presentable version?" Money, fame, and
the wish to prove a point are all acceptable answers here. The skill
records each finding the way the book requires: not as a strength or
a weakness, but as a *fact of your personality*. For example,
"competitive" helps you on a sales call. It hurts you in a team
meeting. The skill records the contexts where each fact helps and
where it quietly hurts.

## Example invocation

You can invoke the skill like this:

```text
/asb-who-me I'm deciding what company to start next, and I keep
picking ideas that impress other founders instead of ideas I'd
actually love running for a decade. Help me figure out who I am first.
```

The skill interviews you in short rounds. Each round has a prompt, an
episode, and a follow-up question. It builds `WHO-ME.md` as you go.
The file records your drives, your dreads, and the tensions between
them. It also records homework for friends who can see what you
cannot see.

One practical note: tell the skill where you are working. Name a
directory, or point to your existing files, when you start the skill.
The skill then keeps all the method's files together in that
location. If you do not name a location, the skill asks before it
creates anything.

## From the source

Two sources form the foundation of this skill:

- As in Chapter 11 of *Hidden Multipliers*: *To Thine Own Self Be True* (sections "Finding Yourself" and "Outside-In") — [hiddenmultipliers.com](https://hiddenmultipliers.com) — this chapter supplies the drive prompts, the anti-questions, and the three outside-in questions. It also supplies the rule that each attribute is a fact. Whether it is a strength or a weakness depends on the context.
- [**Pivot Points**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/pivot-points/) — this published article explains why "strengths and weaknesses" is the wrong frame. It gives the pivot-foot image for building around your fixed facts. It also explains when to invest in changing a fact — rarely.

Supporting articles each add one part of the method:

- [**Finding Fulfillment**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/fulfillment/) — this article describes the Joy/Skill/Need circles and their traps. It is the source of this skill's energized-or-wrecked check, which finds burnout that hides behind good results.
- [**Being who you are, while becoming better**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/be-yourself/) — this article gives the counterweight: accepting your facts does not mean you stop trying to improve. The drive to improve — the "motor" — is itself a trait worth recording.
