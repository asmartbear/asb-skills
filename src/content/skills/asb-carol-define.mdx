## What this is about

"Women over forty-five" was the demographic profile of a certain
beloved sitcom's audience. This profile missed something. The show's
fastest growing fans were young men who loved sharp, character-driven
insult comedy. Demographics often describe your market this way. They
feel close enough to be right. But they are wrong enough to waste every
dollar you aim with them. The book offers a different method: define
Carol — your ideal customer — by the circumstances, behaviors, and
attitudes that actually predict buying, paying, and staying. Do not use
a label like "WordPress users above $1M revenue." Instead, use *a
person for whom a faster website directly increases revenue, and who
can calculate the dollar value of a hundred milliseconds.*

This skill is the final step of the find-your-Carol workshop. It reads
everything the earlier steps produced: classified strengths and
weaknesses, honed keystones, deal-breakers and the anti-market, and
inciting events. It combines these into one crisp definition. It tests
every marker with the keep-asking-why chain (speed matters — to whom?
why?). Each marker must clear a bar. Marketing must be able to target
it. Sales must be able to qualify against it. Product must be able to
build features that thrill it. Sometimes the keystone segments point in
different directions. When this happens, the skill makes you choose
one. It does not let you average them into a definition that fits
nobody. The file leads with the definition, because that is the point.
Brief reference sections on keystones, deal-breakers, and inciting
events follow. Positioning, marketing, and product work will use these
details later.

## Example invocation

You can invoke the skill like this:

```text
/asb-carol-define All four files are done — strengths, keystones
(honed), deal-breakers, inciting events. Write our Carol.
```

The skill drafts the complete CAROL.md. The definition comes first. Reference sections follow. Then the skill reviews the file with you, section by section. It rejects demographic shortcuts and generic markers. It keeps working until every line gives your team something to act on.

One practical note: tell the skill where you are working. Name a
directory, or point to your existing files, when you invoke it. The
skill then keeps all method files together in that location. If you do
not say, the skill asks before it creates anything.

## From the source

Two sources are the foundation for this skill:

- As in Chapter 3 of *Hidden Multipliers*: *Speaking Carol Into Existence* (sections "Beyond Demographics" and "Meet Carol") — [hiddenmultipliers.com](https://hiddenmultipliers.com) — it gives the standard for behavioral definitions, the keep-asking-why chain, the qualifier and disqualifier questions, and the five-step summary this final step assembles.
- As in Chapter 2 of *Hidden Multipliers*: *Carol Loves You for You* — [hiddenmultipliers.com](https://hiddenmultipliers.com) — it explains the Carol/Diana/Eddie bullseye. Targeting only Carol wins a market ten to one hundred times her size. The definition file states this as the strategic case.

Each supporting source supplies one piece of the method:

- [**Selling to Carol: Why targeting an ICP brings 10x more customers than you expected**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/icp-ideal-customer-persona/) — this article gives the published form of the whole argument this file puts into practice.
- [**"It's a Balance" isn't always the answer**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/conflicting-choices/) — this explains why diverging keystone segments force a choice, not an average. A Carol defined as the middle of two segments describes nobody.
- [**Specificity: A weapon of mass effectiveness**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/specificity/) — this sets the wording bar for every marker. Vague definitions cannot be targeted, qualified, or built for.
- [**Choosing a target market**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/target-market/) — this lists non-demographic dimensions (decision cycle, problem ownership, emergency vs. deliberate, and two dozen more). Test each candidate marker against this list, and use it to find more markers. Avoid demographic dimensions. These dimensions predict buying instead.
