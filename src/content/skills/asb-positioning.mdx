## What this is about

The same product can sell for eight times more, depending on how you
describe it. "Cut your ad spend in half" limits your price to a share
of the savings. "Double your leads at the same cost per lead" prices
against growth instead — and the buyer will happily pay several times
more, because growth is what their CEO celebrates. That is the book's
first positioning rule: never sell saved time or money. Sell **more
value, in the terms your customer already uses to measure value**.
Most copy breaks this rule in the first line. Two subtler breaks never
use the word "save" at all: copy where every sentence describes how
the product works, and copy that promises what *arrives* — a report,
a score, a reading — instead of what changes. Technical founders write
both kinds of copy.

The second rule: a vague true statement says nothing. Take "Easy to
use, robust features, customer-first." The Opposite Test destroys this
claim — no one says their product is *difficult* — and generic words
let the reader skim past without forming a picture. Vivid copy is
specific copy: say "$300 an hour" instead of "expensive," give the
benchmark instead of "fast," give the changelog instead of
"innovative," say the quiet part out loud.

This skill applies the full method to your actual copy, one message at
a time. It finds what rule each message breaks, then rewrites it with
you, asking for real numbers and proof. It never invents a specific
fact — missing facts go on a list to verify later. The statements that
pass build up into a reusable list covering every role: what you do,
what you promise, what you make unnecessary, how you answer vendors
trying to make you unnecessary, and the inciting-event language that
makes a reader say "that's me." The skill works much better if you
also bring your ideal-customer definition and needs stack — it will
tell you so — but it also works without them.

## What it's looking for

The skill checks every message against all of these, in order. A
statement that fails any check is never recorded:

- **Savings framing.** "Cut your ad spend in half" limits your price
  to a share of the savings. Unless your reader is *paid* on that
  number, the skill reframes the claim around what the reader is
  actually measured on.
- **Mechanism pitches.** Every sentence describes how the product
  works. This is the most common mistake technical founders make.
- **Deliverable pitches.** The pitch describes what arrives — a
  report, a score, a dashboard — instead of what changes. No one is
  graded on receiving a document.
- **Wrong level.** A feature description used as an aspiration, or a
  promise many levels above what the product actually does. You can
  credibly claim only one level above what the product does.
- **Clauses that widen instead of narrowing.** "New revenue *or* more
  customers," "for startups and enterprises alike," "…and more." Each
  added clause looks to you like more capability. To your reader, it
  looks like less. Keep only the clauses that narrow the claim.
- **A mechanism sitting *beside* an outcome.** "Turns client problems
  into AI products **and** new revenue" puts what you build next to
  what changes, as if they were equal. One is a means to the other,
  and belongs below it, not beside it.
- **Generic words.** Fast, powerful, easy, robust, secure, "with AI" —
  the skill replaces each word with the specific fact behind it.
- **Clichés.** Leading provider, cutting-edge, game-changing,
  world-class, proven track record. 78% of "About Us" pages claim to
  be the leading provider of something.
- **Telling where you could show.** "It's fast" becomes the benchmark
  number; "we innovate" becomes the public changelog; "legendary
  support" becomes "we're a small team, you get us personally."
- **Claims no competitor would contradict.** The Opposite Test: if no
  one would reasonably choose the opposite, you have said nothing.
- **Mixed price signals.** The skill asks which of the book's three
  pricing strategies you use — More for More, More for Less, or Less
  for Less — and holds every claim to that one story, because
  "highest quality" next to "cheapest" confuses the buyer, and a
  confused buyer walks away.
- **Invented specifics.** Numbers, benchmarks, and testimonials must
  come from you, or they do not go in — a placeholder and a note to
  verify hold the spot until the real figure lands.
- **Gaps in coverage.** No claim about what you make unnecessary, no
  answer to vendors trying to make you unnecessary, no inciting-event
  language that makes a reader say "that's me."

## Example invocation

You can invoke the skill like this:

```text
/asb-positioning Here's our homepage hero and three ad variants. We
sell automated backup for dental practices — current headline is
"Enterprise-grade backup, made easy." Our CAROL.md and NEEDS-STACK.md
are in this directory too.
```

The skill works through your messages one at a time. It checks each
one against the value-framing, level-fit, and vividness rules, then
rewrites it using your real facts. You end with a `POSITIONING.md`
file of statements sharp enough to reuse in every channel.

One practical note: tell the skill where you are working. Name a
directory, or point to your existing files, when you invoke it. The
skill then keeps the method's files together there. If you do not
name a location, it asks before it creates anything.

- [**How repositioning a product allows you to 8x its price**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/more-value-not-save-money/) — the value-framing rule in full: why the same buyer values growth at ten times the price of savings, and why the pitch, not the product, sets the price.

Supporting articles each supply one part of the method:

- [**Specificity: A weapon of mass effectiveness**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/specificity/) — the source of vivid copy: it turns generic words into specifics, turns true statements into accurate ones, uses concrete examples, and shows how specific copy is even funnier.
- [**The "Opposite Test"**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/opposite-test/) — the claim filter: if no competitor would claim the opposite, you are saying nothing. A claim that passes this test has consequences — name them and accept them.
- [**"Authentic" is dead. And so is "is dead."**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/authentic-is-dead/) — the list of dead clichés this skill enforces, plus two ways to redeem a word: name it and accept its cost, or own an overused word by backing it with strong proof.
- As in Chapter 6 of *Hidden Multipliers*: *Price Is Not Just a Number* (section "Mixed Signals") — [hiddenmultipliers.com](https://hiddenmultipliers.com) — the three consistent pricing strategies (More for More, More for Less, Less for Less) this skill holds every claim to. When price and positioning tell different stories, customers get confused, and confused customers do not buy.
- [**Using the Needs Stack for competitive strategy**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/needs-stack/) — the level-fit framework: describe features at your level, promise one level up, and position against the vendors trying to make you unnecessary.
- [**Selling to Carol: Why targeting an ICP brings 10x more customers than you expected**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/icp-ideal-customer-persona/) — who the copy is for: the keystones and inciting events this skill uses for headlines come from the ideal-customer work.
