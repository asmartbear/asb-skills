## What this is about

You must know who you are before you can define the customer who loves
you for it. But the instruction "write down our strengths and
weaknesses" does not work. Nobody knows where to start. Everyone makes
excuses. The loudest voice fills the whiteboard. The book's answer is to
start one level lower, with raw observations. You gather these through
twelve detailed, vivid question categories. Examples: what customers
praise when they choose you despite your faults; the complaint you
cannot defend; what you are proud of, whether customers care or not;
what separates your most profitable customers from the rest; what you
claim to do well but do not; what you envy in competitors; what you
would keep even if it cost you money.

This skill runs that fact-finding as a working session. If you bring a
team's write-storm output, it processes the notes one observation at a
time. It clarifies each one, removes duplicates, and sharpens the
wording. If you bring nothing but yourself, it walks you through the
twelve categories directly. It presses every vague answer into a
specific, vivid fact. For example, it turns "support could be better"
into "customers post screenshots of support wait times on Reddit." If
your company already operates and strangers already talk about it
online, the skill first scans that public record. It checks reviews,
forum threads, press, and competitor comparisons. It files what it finds
in an External Research section. This section seeds the walk and
sharpens its prompts. These findings stay as candidates until you
confirm them. A pre-launch company with no public footprint skips this
scan. Two rules hold throughout, both from the book. First, observe like
an outside consultant: record behaviors and outcomes, never
justifications. Second, do not evaluate, blame, or act yet. Judging what
is a strength or weakness is the next step's job. This step only gets
the truth on the record.

## Example invocation

You can invoke the skill like this:

```text
/asb-carol-observations We're a 6-person bootstrapped SaaS doing email
deliverability monitoring, 4 years in, ~$50k MRR. I want to figure out
who our ideal customer really is — start with the honest look at us.
```

The skill walks you through the twelve question categories one at a time. It presses each answer into a specific, vivid observation and records it in `OBSERVATIONS.md` as you go. You end with the raw material. The next step, strengths-and-weaknesses, uses this material.

One practical note: say where you are working. Name a directory, or
point at your existing files, when you invoke the skill. The skill then
keeps the method's files together there. If you do not say, it asks
before it creates anything.

- [**Selling to Carol: Why targeting an ICP brings 10x more customers than you expected**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/icp-ideal-customer-persona/) — why this exercise exists: the ideal-customer definition that everything here feeds.

Supporting sources each supply a piece of the mechanism:

- *Write-Storming Workshop* (appendix) in *Hidden Multipliers* — [hiddenmultipliers.com](https://hiddenmultipliers.com) — brainstorm alone, process together: the workshop mechanics this skill's team mode follows and its solo mode adapts.
- [**Specificity: A weapon of mass effectiveness**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/specificity/) — why every observation must be specific and vivid: different readers interpret generic words in different ways, so generic words give no evidence.
- [**Fail to face the truth, and you fail**](https://longform.asmartbear.com/failure-to-face-the-truth/) — the posture behind the whole exercise: look honestly at what is true, then decide what to do about it.
